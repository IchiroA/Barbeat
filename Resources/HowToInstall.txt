Barbeat
=======

Turns your Mac's menu bar, and the rest of your screen, into a light show that moves with whatever you're playing.

Made by Ichiro Ashihara (@IchiroA). © 2026 Ichiro Ashihara. Open source under the MIT License.

Needs: a Mac with Apple Silicon (M1 or newer) running macOS 14.2 Sonoma or later. Intel Macs aren't supported.
Optional: a camera or HDMI capture device macOS sees without a driver (UVC), or an iPhone as a Continuity Camera;
a Philips Hue Bridge (the square v2 model) with an Entertainment area, or WLED / Art-Net lights on your network.

Install
1. Drag "Barbeat" into your Applications folder.
2. Open it. The first time, macOS checks that you want to open an app downloaded from the internet. Click Open.
   A small bar-chart icon appears in the menu bar (there's no Dock icon).
3. macOS asks to let Barbeat record system audio. Click Allow.
   It only listens so it can animate the menu bar. Nothing is recorded or saved.
4. macOS also asks to let Barbeat record the screen. Allow it to have the colors follow what's on screen
   (if macOS offers "Quit & Reopen", click it). Barbeat only reads a few tiny frames a second for their
   colors; nothing is recorded or saved. Without it, the colors use your Mac's accent color, or pick
   any other palette in the menu bar panel.
5. Play some music. It starts with stereo Center bars in your screen's colors and a glow around the screen edges.

If macOS won't open the app
Open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click "Open Anyway" next to Barbeat.

New in 2.2
Screen colors and the kaleidoscope can watch a camera or capture device instead of the screen: a game console,
Apple TV or Blu-ray player on an HDMI capture device, a webcam, or your iPhone (Settings > Screen colors > What it
watches; macOS asks for camera access the first time). Barbeat also rests more while nothing plays.

New in 2.1
Choose which edges of the screen glow (Settings > Glow and screens > Sides). The kaleidoscope now covers the
whole screen, menu bar included (point at the top of the screen to bring the menu bar back), stays on the
desktop you started it on, and keeps up with video playing underneath. Switching desktops no longer makes
Barbeat stall.

New in 2.0
Beat pulse: the whole screen pulses on every beat (a glow from the edges, a wash of color, or the screen's
brightness), locked to the song's tempo. Kaleidoscope: a show mode that turns your screen into a kaleidoscope
moving with the music; click anywhere or press Esc to stop it. Both are at the bottom of the menu bar panel.
The kaleidoscope uses the same screen recording permission as Screen colors; frames go straight to the
graphics chip and are thrown away.

Settings
Click the bar-chart icon in the menu bar for quick controls, and Settings... for everything else.
Pick a style (LED, Bars, Center, Glow, Pulse, VU, Aura, Waterfall, Scope, Sparks), colors (Screen colors follow what's on screen; or make your own under Custom),
color drift, Stereo or full-width layout, brightness, Low/Mid/High boost, spark power, the screen edge glow
(which edges, and how far it reaches into the screen), Keep menu bar text readable, whether to show over full-screen
apps (just the edge glow, or the menu bar visuals too), and Launch at login.

Listen to: everything your Mac plays, just one app (say Spotify, so calls and alerts don't move the bars),
or a microphone / audio input. The first time you pick an input, macOS asks for microphone access.
A capture device's sound is one click away under Screen colors > What it watches ("Listen to Its Sound Too").

If nothing moves
Make sure Barbeat is allowed under System Settings > Privacy & Security >
Screen & System Audio Recording, then click "Try Again" in the menu bar panel.
